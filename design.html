02 / Combat & systems design

Strife & Serenity

A playable action RPG prologue built around swordplay, defensive actions and elemental abilities. My focus here is how those tools become a readable kit, and how the gauge gives players a reason to keep using their abilities.

From inspiration to implementation

Kingdom Hearts Birth by Sleep’s Command Gauge inspired mine. Its ability combinations can lead to specific Command Styles; my prologue uses a simpler, linear progression.

The combat gauge

Use the kit
Build your momentum

Using any ability fills the gauge. As the combat level rises, abilities become stronger, giving players an active way to build power during a fight.

Ability use → fill the gauge → higher combat level → stronger abilities.

The prologue uses a linear progression across three combat levels. My intention is to keep players on their toes and make ability use central to overcoming enemies.

Systems design · Customisation

Enhance the kit

Marks reinforce the player’s capabilities. My earlier direction explored tying spells to equipment slots; for the prologue, I kept core abilities available and used Marks to enhance what the player could already do.

Customisation inspiration

Inspired by Materia

Marks are loosely inspired by Final Fantasy VII’s Materia system and its approach to customisation. I adapted that inspiration around S&S’s existing kit: keeping core abilities available, with Marks building on them.

Borrowed advice & the crossing

Borrowing advice changes traversal without committing a route. Falls retain the current advice for another attempt; reflecting after a crossing records the approach and returns the player to the courtyard.

Talking and trying advice keep the route open. The player can back out of the offer or confirm the speaking voice’s route, which ends the opening slice.

EXPERIMENT 01 / UNITY PROTOTYPE

Echo

Can the player solve a puzzle through their past self?

Record a movement sequence, then let its echo perform it. Each recording can be replayed once, so completing the required order depends on planning before the playback. The poem-led puzzle takes inspiration from Stonespoke: the poem tells you which sequence of panels to hit.

Design hypothesisClear feedback and a single-use constraint will encourage players to plan ahead instead of reacting in the moment.

Original development log

Tobi Adefidiya Echo is my prototype that explores whether a puzzle game can be built around absence rather than presence. Instead of directly interacting with the world to progress, the player records their own movements and relies on an echo of those past actions to solve puzzles. This prototype is built around the theme “what remains”, asking the player to think ahead and consider the consequences of actions. Build Notes: Question: Can a puzzle be solved by removing the player from the action and forcing them to rely on their past self? Hypothesis: If a player is given clear feedback and constraints, they will adapt to solving problems indirectly by planning their actions in advance rather than reacting in the moment. Inputs: Keyboard movement (WASD) Record action (R) Replay echo (E) Additional instructions/useful info: Players must record their movement before attempting to progress. Echoes can only be used once per recording, and puzzles require completing actions in a specific order during a single playback. EXPERIMENT 02 / UNITY PROTOTYPE

Crossfire Chaos

How overwhelming can a simple task feel while still being possible?

A straightforward objective becomes harder under time pressure and active opposition. The design keeps the action simple while asking the player to keep moving and stay aware of the arena.

Design hypothesisLayering time pressure and direct opposition over a simple objective will make an achievable task feel chaotic and daunting.

Original development log

Tobi Adefidiya Crossfire Chaos is my prototype that explores how a simple objective can be made overwhelming through pressure and opposition. The player is tasked with a straightforward goal, but the addition of time constraints and active threats creates a sense of chaos and urgency. The intention was to make the task feel almost impossible at first glance, while still being achievable through persistence and determination. Build Notes Question: Can a task be made to feel chaotic and daunting while still remaining possible? Hypothesis: If a simple objective is layered with time pressure and direct opposition, players will perceive the challenge as overwhelming even though the underlying task remains achievable. Inputs: Keyboard movement (WASD) Swing Hammer (LMB/Left Mouse Button) Additional instructions/useful info: Players are encouraged to keep moving and stay aware of their surroundings. The experience is designed to feel intense and stressful, even though the core objective itself is simple.
